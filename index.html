How to use this workbook

A few answers before you begin.

How do I use it?

One tool per day, in the order the emails follow. Each day's email opens one tool. Do that page before the next email arrives, and try not to skip ahead. One honest page a day is the whole method.

Does my work save?

Yes, automatically, in this browser on this device. Close it and come back anytime. Your answers are stored on your device, so they do not sync to other devices. If you want a copy you can keep anywhere, use Print and save it as a PDF.

How do I print it or save a PDF?

Use the Print / Save PDF button at the top right, or your browser's print command, then choose "Save as PDF." Printing shows the full workbook, all nine tools, with your typed answers included.

What if I miss a day?

Nothing breaks. The workbook waits. Pick it back up with one honest page when you can.

I'm ready for more than a workbook. What's next?

When you want company for the decision, a Next Chapter Clarity Conversation is the next step. The link is just below.

What is The Identity Reset?

A free 9-day email course. Nine mistakes keeping you in a life that looks right and feels off, one a day. This workbook is its companion, holding all nine tools in one place.

Is this therapy?

No. This is identity work. Therapy looks back to heal. This looks at who you have been performing as and who you actually are, and closes that gap. It is not productivity or mindset coaching either. Different problem, different tool.

What if I can't name what's wrong?

You do not need to name it first. Most women who start here cannot explain what is wrong, only that something is off. Naming it is the work. These tools are built to help you put words to it.

I don't have time for this.

This is built for women with no spare time. It is ten honest minutes a day. The point is alignment, not more on your plate.

How is this different from other coaching?

Most coaching treats the symptom: burnout, discipline, strategy, mindset. This treats the cause, which is identity. You cannot optimize your way out of an identity problem. That is the whole difference, and it is why the other things did not hold.

About

Nijah Almond

Nijah Almond spent more than fifteen years in government and corporate leadership. She earned the degrees, the titles, and the life she was supposed to want. Diagnosed with lupus at nine, she learned early that time is not promised.

Decades later, in a room she had fought to get into, she realized the life she had built no longer reflected the woman she had become. She named that distance the identity gap. Today she helps high-performing executive women close it, so they can decide whether their next chapter calls for reinvention or recommitment, from truth instead of fear. This workbook is where it starts.
